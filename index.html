www.world_wide_web

A pendant lamp that spins a web of light across the room, connecting the people beneath it the way the Internet connects the world.

CourseDesign Studio II
InstructorProf. Sangmin Bae
ToolsRhino 7, Gemini for references
Output3D-printed, working lamp · 2026

A web wrapped around the world

Spider webs come together into a sphere: the Earth, held together by the network that connects everyone on it. When the bulb turns on, the web's shadow falls across the walls and onto everyone in the room.

FormSphere = Earth

Webs rotated into a sphere stand for the whole world.

StructureWeb = Network

Threads are connections spread across the globe.

LightGlow = Bond

The lit web falls on people and ties them together.

“Once you enter the Internet, it’s hard to get away, like anything caught in a spider web.”
Personal interpretation behind the concept

Radial rotation, three times

A spider builds its web by circling one centre. The lamp repeats that motion: the spider spins outward thread by thread, one web is rotated copy after copy until it closes into a sphere, and light radiates from the centre until the shadow reaches the walls.

AIGemini helped with mood images and geometry references. The 3D model was built by hand in Rhino 7, then 3D-printed as a fully working lamp.

H-Dry

A hair dryer that starts from what people dislike about the ones they already own.

CourseComputer-Aided Design, final project
InstructorProf. Jieun Shim
Tools3D CAD, KeyShot 10
OutputRenders, assembly, exploded drawing · 2026
Benchmark 01too bulky

Heavy, oversized heads.

Benchmark 02ordinary design

Shapes that all look alike.

Benchmark 03too short

Barrels that end too soon.

A slim barrel you can see into

H-Dry answers those three notes with a long, slim barrel and a smoked translucent shell, so the heating coil and the circuit board stay visible inside. The handle drops straight down from the body.

Every part was modeled, assembled and rendered in KeyShot, then documented as an exploded technical drawing with a parts list.

Circuit de Monaco Perfectionniste

An award for the driver who sets a lap record at Monaco. The circuit itself becomes the trophy.

CourseComputer-Aided Design, midterm
InstructorProf. Jieun Shim
Tools3D CAD, KeyShot 10
OutputRenders, drawing at 1:1 · 2026

Precision and speed, in one line

Monaco is one of the most unforgiving tracks in racing: almost no run-off, no margin for error, and precision held at constant speed for the whole race. The trophy follows the track outline, with PRECISION and SPEED worked into the corners where each one matters most.

Mirror-polished metal picks up the colours around it, so the trophy changes with the room it stands in.

Mountain

The Wikipedia article on mountains, reset as a two-page A3 spread that you read by climbing it.

TypeEditorial information design
SourceWikipedia, “Mountain”
Format2 × A3, print
Structure8 sections, 16 references

The layout is the landform

Each text column sits one step higher than the last, so the page itself becomes an elevation profile. Altitude markers run up the side, key figures are pulled out at the height where they belong, and a small diagram shows how vegetation changes from forest to bare rock and ice.

8,849 m

Height of Mount Everest, set at the top of the climb.

−6.5 °C

Average temperature drop for every 1,000 m climbed.

~24 %

Share of Earth’s land surface that is mountainous.

0 → 3,000 m

Altitude scale the columns are set against.
